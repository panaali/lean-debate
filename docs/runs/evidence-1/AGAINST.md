# Evidence run 1: Agent AGAINST (K1)

Assignment: K1 is false or does not do the work claimed. Cutoff: June 2026. All
probabilities are subjective, evidence-weighted.

Headline: K1 is *literally* true of tribal Indians, and *Elk* itself concedes it ("not
merely subject in some respect or degree"). But the ground of exclusion is
intersovereign (tribal quasi-sovereignty, treaties, immunity of intra-tribal conduct),
which *Wong Kim Ark* (1898) and *Trump v. Barbara* (June 30, 2026) expressly refuse to
extend to children of unauthorized immigrants. K1 refutes J17 only in its crudest form.

## 3. Exact disputed wording and plausible readings

**(a) *Elk* at 102, "not merely subject in some respect or degree ... but completely
subject to their political jurisdiction."** Reading 1 (FOR): a general test — partial
answerability is insufficient; a further quantum (allegiance/consent/domicile) is
required. Reading 2 (AGAINST): a description of persons whose internal affairs are
governed by another polity the US treats with by treaty. Context favours 2: the sentence
sits between "alien nations, distinct political communities" and the ambassador analogy,
and *Elk*'s author limited it to tribes in *WKA* 682.

**(b) Trumbull, Globe 2893, "Not owing allegiance to anybody else."** Reading 1: a
requirement beyond legal subjection. Reading 2: the same speech glosses it — "Can you
sue a Navajoe Indian in court? ... We make treaties with them, and therefore they are
not subject to our jurisdiction ... It is only those persons who come completely within
our jurisdiction, who are subject to our laws, that we think of making citizens." He
defines allegiance by legal subjection, not the reverse.

**(c) Williams, Globe 2897: all born here "are subject to the jurisdiction ... but ...
not ... in every sense," e.g. an ambassador's child "could be tried for murder."** The
strongest drafting-record sentence for K1 literally: triability does not make one
"subject to the jurisdiction." But both examples are immunities the US grants to another
sovereign; no immigrant is named.

**(d) *WKA*, "while domiciled here" / "permitted by the United States to reside here."**
Reading 1 (FOR): holding limited to domiciled, permitted residents. Reading 2: rule
stated as covering "all children here born of resident aliens" with a closed list of
exceptions; *Barbara* pp. 24–25 rejects the domicile limit. Reading 1 has never carried
a majority.

**(e) SETUP wording "subject to criminal prosecution by United States authorities."**
Reading 1: some members could be prosecuted for some crimes (true — §2145; state law
off reservation). Reading 2: generally subject to US criminal law as residents are
(false — §2146, *Crow Dog*). K1 needs Reading 2 for the immigrant analogy; the record
supports only Reading 1.

## 5. Strongest counterargument to my side

"*Elk* holds that a person born on US soil, whom the United States could and did try
for crimes against its citizens and who later lived under Nebraska law, was not 'subject
to the jurisdiction' at birth. The Court did not say 'immune'; it said 'not merely
subject in some respect or degree.' Senator Williams said the same in 1866: an
ambassador's child 'could be tried for murder' and is still outside the clause. So the
clause cannot mean 'answerable to our law'; it means a complete, reciprocal political
relation — allegiance. *Wong Kim Ark* confined *Elk* in one sentence and decided only
the case of parents with 'a permanent domicil and residence' whom the United States had
'permitted ... to reside here.' The 2026 Court needed a fiction of 'ceded' jurisdiction
to explain why tribes but not unlawful entrants fall outside; four Justices found no
principled line. On the historical question — did the ratifiers require more than
answerability — *Elk* and the Globe say yes, and that is all K1 asserts."

Serious; strongest on K1a–K1c, weakest on K1d, where it must replace the drafters'
stated ground (treaties, tribal self-government, "quasi foreign nations") with an
unstated one.
