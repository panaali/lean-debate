## 1. Motion, date, moderator, debaters

- **Motion (as stated by the moderator, repeatedly):** "Abolish the Death Penalty." (The Substack post is titled "Should the Death Penalty Be Abolished?"; the motion voted on is the imperative form.)
- **Date:** The Substack transcript post is dated 2026-07-23 (page metadata). The page does not state the event date. Internal references (the debate is "in the wake of the Boston Marathon conviction"; Anthony Hinton "walked off death row in Alabama" in "early April"; the next debate announced for "May 13" is "Smart Technology Is Making Us Dumb"; 18 states abolished, 15 suspended, 17 retain) place the live event in April 2015 at the Kaufman Music Center, New York City. Treat "April 2015" as inferred, not stated on the page.
- **Moderator:** John Donvan. (Robert Rosenkranz, chairman of Intelligence Squared U.S., gives a pre-debate framing conversation; he is not a debater.)
- **For the motion (abolish):**
  - Barry Scheck — co-founder/co-director of the Innocence Project; professor, Cardozo School of Law.
  - Diann Rust-Tierney — executive director, National Coalition to Abolish the Death Penalty.
- **Against the motion (retain the death penalty):**
  - Kent Scheidegger — legal director, Criminal Justice Legal Foundation.
  - Robert Blecker — professor, New York Law School; author of "The Death of Punishment."
- **Format:** three rounds (7-minute openings; moderated exchange with audience questions; 2-minute closings). Audience votes before and after; the side that gains the most percentage points wins.

## 3. Claimed inferences

Format: speaker — premises (claim numbers) => conclusion — signal words.

1. **BS** — BS3, BS4, BS6, BS7, BS8 (costs) + BS1 (cost/benefit framing) => BS2 (death penalty doesn't work; repeal). Signal: "when you reason about this as a public policy issue... and you look at the costs and benefits... you're going to reach the same conclusion."

## 5. Unstated premises (tentative)

- **BS/DRT cost-benefit framing (BS1, BS2, DRT15):** assumes that retributive desert either has no weight or can be treated as one benefit to be traded off against costs; Blecker (RB34) explicitly denies this. Not defended by the For side beyond asserting it is a "public policy" question.
- **BS4 => BS21:** assumes proponents rest their case on deterrence; Blecker (RB12) and Scheidegger (KS27) both say justice alone suffices, so the inference needs the further premise that a purely desert-based justification is insufficient for public policy.
- **BS5, DRT2 (cross-jurisdiction rate comparisons):** need the premise that other determinants of homicide are comparable across the compared jurisdictions (Scheidegger's KS20 attacks exactly this).
- **BS11/BS12 (4.1 percent => unacceptable):** needs a premise about what error threshold is acceptable and that capital error is comparable to aviation error; also assumes the Gross estimate measures wrongful conviction rather than a bound or a projection.
- **BS3/BS15 (risk of executing innocents => abolish):** needs the premise that the risk cannot be reduced to an acceptable level by reform (Scheck asserts BS34 in closing; Rust-Tierney asserts DRT13), plus the premise that LWOP carries less irreversible harm to innocents (contested by KS16).
- **KS4 (incapacitation):** needs the premise that murders by lifers could not be prevented by prison security (Blecker's RB12 says they can), and that the count of such murders exceeds wrongful executions.
- **KS5 (incentives matter => deterrence):** needs the premise that prospective murderers perceive and weigh the marginal difference between death and LWOP; DRT5 and BS30 deny this for the relevant population; KS32 supplies a partial answer.
- **KS16 / RB29 (innocents better off on death row):** needs the premise that funded post-conviction review for death-sentenced inmates would not be extended to lifers under abolition, and that the review process catches errors before execution.
- **KS12/KS28 (obstruction costs are not "costs of the death penalty"):** needs the premise that the appeals and delays are avoidable without raising the error rate—i.e., that "six years" would preserve accuracy.
- **KS13 (most capital cases have no doubt of guilt):** needs the premise that certainty of guilt can be identified ex ante for those cases, which is the reliability question Scheck disputes (BS15).
- **DRT8-DRT10 (race-of-victim effect => abolish):** needs the premise that a disparity in whom the penalty is sought for is a reason to abolish rather than to equalize (Blecker RB21/RB22 and Scheidegger KS21 push toward the alternative reading).
- **DRT4/DRT28 (police chiefs' ranking):** needs the premise that chiefs' resource priorities are the right measure of a punishment's value—Blecker's RB27 denies this.
- **DRT25 (abolition advances human rights abroad):** needs the premise that foreign governments' practices are responsive to U.S. example.
- **DRT31 (all humans capable of change):** needs the premise that the justice system's purpose includes rehabilitation for the worst offenders, which Blecker denies for the enumerated class.
- **RB8-RB10 (desert can be defined and enumerated in advance):** needs the premise that juries and prosecutors will apply the definition without the arbitrariness and race effects the For side describes; RB22/RB23 partially concede the problem.
- **RB14/RB17 (accept small risks for justice):** needs the premise that executing an innocent is a risk of the same moral kind as an accidental death (a state deliberately killing vs. a truck jumping a curb), and that the actual risk is "infinitesimal" (i.e., RB18 must be true).
- **RB19/RB20 (D.C. and the inmate):** needs the premise that a single omitted jurisdiction or single case is probative against aggregate studies.
- **RB21 (alternative explanations):** needs the premise that victims' families' wishes drive charging decisions, and that lower Black community support explains prosecutorial patterns.
- **RB28 (media causes abolition):** needs the premise that legislators respond to headlines rather than to the underlying data.
- **RB1-RB5 (LWOP is not punishment):** needs the premise that punishment must be experienced as suffering beyond loss of liberty; RB31 concedes loss of liberty is punishment, so the argument requires a proportionality premise that this is "not enough" for the worst cases.
- **Both sides on "the worst of the worst":** the Against side needs the premise that the current system, or a reformed one, actually confines the penalty to that class; the For side's DRT6/DRT7 and BS7 point to concentration by geography rather than by crime severity, but neither side states the sorting claim directly.
