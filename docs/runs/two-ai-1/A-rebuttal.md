## The reading of P

Position, typed INTERPRETIVE (entered as A9; A1 is withdrawn as a DEFINITION and re-entered as A9): P's "born in the United States" is BornInUSConst.

Strongest reason: "born in the United States" is the Clause's own phrase, and B himself reads it as BornInUSConst when it appears in L1 (B-opening, symbol demand 1). B reads the same words two ways in one signature. Second reason: under B's reading P's qualifier "to parents without lawful immigration status" does no work. B's witness would lack citizenship with lawful-status parents or US-national parents exactly as with undocumented ones; B has refuted "every person born on US soil is a citizen at birth," which nobody proposed. A proposition whose only contested variable is inert in its refutation has been misread.

I acknowledge that the signature's fixed gloss for BornInUS is territorial; that is B's best ground.

Concession: yes. Under the territorial reading I concede NOT-P. Given B1 (near-one probability) and B4 (current construction), IB1 and IB2 are valid and P_territory is false. I do not fight on "immigration status" meaning INA status only: if "United States" is read to include American Samoa, "immigration status" must be read to include its regime, which B's gloss already does.
