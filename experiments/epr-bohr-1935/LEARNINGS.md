# Learnings from the EPR–Bohr conversion

This experiment produced two readable companions, 44 checked theorems, and 33 report
entries. Its strongest result is a precise account of which assumptions connect
predictions, reality assignments, and completeness in the chosen reconstructions.

1. **Preserve the disagreement in the vocabulary.** EPR's joint reality and Bohr's
   joint predictive warrant are different propositions. The comparison aligns the
   models' prediction and representation tables without identifying those concepts.
   During review, `CoarseReality` was renamed `CriterionEligible` to avoid calling
   eligibility for a sufficient criterion physical reality itself.

2. **Countermodels reveal more than a successful derivation alone.** EPR's contextual
   countermodel retains the other premises and completeness while rejecting reality
   invariance. Bohr's second scenario retains the predictive critique while failing
   scope completeness. These locate dependencies; they do not demonstrate physically
   realizable alternatives to quantum mechanics.

3. **Definitions can carry substantive assumptions.** Removing the context index
   makes EPR's reality invariance automatic. Bohr's positive completeness example
   succeeds because its representation relation was chosen to match its warrants.
   Neither shorter proofs nor fewer displayed premises establish stronger science.

4. **Audit the actual proofs and the prose separately.** Every report entry carries
   a proof term, but captions and source mappings still need review. Direct arithmetic
   proofs removed dependencies introduced by automation; two identities retain core
   `propext`. The other 42 theorems have no axiom dependencies. This accounting is
   separate from the physical hypotheses written as theorem parameters.

The main criticism is the distance between these finite signatures and the original
physics. Values, quantum states, continuous-variable calculations, and apparatus
dynamics are largely abstracted away. Several proofs are short consequences of the
definitions; their usefulness depends on whether readers accept those definitions.
The compatibility result is therefore limited evidence about the historical dispute.

The delegated source review helped identify interpretive choices, but was neither
blind nor an independent domain-expert assessment. No archived source PDFs accompany
the locators. These two user-selected papers also provide no evidence that the method
scales across fields or improves peer review. A useful next evaluation would compare
independent reconstructions and expert judgments of the exact disputed definitions.
